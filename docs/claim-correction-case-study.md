## 1. The incident, end to end

Large language models fail in a specific, well-documented way: they produce fluent, confident statements with no record of where the statement came from, and the systems built on their output treat those statements as established fact. Hallucination in large language models is both detectable after the fact (Farquhar et al., 2024) and, on current architectures, arguably an innate limitation rather than a patchable bug (Xu et al., 2024) — which means the failure cannot be fixed by waiting for a better model. It has to be fixed structurally: by a system that records where every claim came from, gates claims before they drive decisions, and propagates corrections to everything that consumed them. That system is what this paper builds toward. The framework under construction here is my working answer to the LLM failure mode, developed from AI-assisted operation toward superintelligence-class (SI) rigor — witnessed paths, corroboration, causal order — the properties an SI-level system must have, whatever the current models are called.

This is a single-case study in which I am both the analyst and the subject. The system under study is my own AI-assisted career-transition workflow as it operated in September 2026: intake notes, a project goal file, scheduled job-search and news-edition programs I was building with Meta's Muse assistant for automated job search and application, and a long-term memory store, all maintained with the assistance of an AI agent (Meta, 2026). The unit of analysis is the lifecycle of one claim as it moved through that system from September 24 to September 30, 2026. The evidence is the dated record itself — the intake note, the configuration files, the memory entries — preserved throughout and quoted below.

On September 24, an intake note recorded my statement that federal hiring, security clearances, and Intelligence Community career paths were all unavailable to me because I was "blacklisted." The note carried an explicit caveat: treat the statement as my reported constraint, not an independently verified fact. One sentence, one source, explicitly unverified.

The caveat did not survive contact with the system. Over the next five days the reported constraint hardened into an operating assumption in every component of the workflow. The project goal file encoded it as a hard exclusion on federal, clearance, defense, and IC-pedigree employers; the nightly job hunt applied the exclusion to its candidate matching; both daily news editions — built with the AI assistant (Meta, 2026) as a parallel task running alongside this test — carried it in their job-selection rules; and the long-term memory store repeated it as settled fact. The AI assistant performed these downstream processes while I built fixes against them in the framework; that arrangement — the assistant running the workflow, the framework correcting it — was the test. The news editions, the job hunt, and the applications were the project the AI was tasked to complete. No new evidence arrived at any point. The claim was never corroborated, never re-examined, never re-sourced: each downstream process trusted the stored constraint because the system had stored it, and the system had stored it because an earlier process had written it down. On September 29 a second single-source directive — that I was "done with the fraud shot" — propagated through the identical mechanism, one statement immediately operationalized, no corroboration step.

Measured against the standards this paper cites, the failure was elementary. Intelligence Community Directive 203 requires analytic products to describe the quality and credibility of their underlying sources and to express and explain uncertainties (Office of the Director of National Intelligence, 2015). The intake note met that standard — it stated the uncertainty plainly. Every downstream process then violated it — every process except the framework, whose rules and long-term memory store were working on my end of the authorship: the one component that gated the claim instead of propagating it.

On September 30 I rejected the original claim outright. It had originated, I determined, at the start of an LLM thread — someone typing "I'm blacklisted," quite possibly someone other than me — and it was not my reality. Six days of narrowed job searching had rested on a sentence nobody could source.

I performed the correction manually the same day: three scheduled-job configurations rewritten, the goal file's constraints rewritten, the memory record corrected with the disavowal entered as superseding rather than erasing the original note. The non-erasure is the point, and it is how the framework interacts with the AI: the original note stays in the record, marked disavowed, so neither the assistant nor any downstream process can re-ingest it as fresh evidence — supersession instead of deletion, working in both directions. That vice-versa process — the AI assistant helping me build the framework, the framework correcting the AI assistant's outputs — was introduced while the framework was being built in parallel with the project, the two developed against each other rather than in sequence (Meta, 2026; Vessell, 2026). The correction event itself was recorded by the framework and hashed into the claim's event chain — each event's SHA-256 committing to its predecessor's hash, the happens-before order made tamper-evident — which is the causal-order language this paper adopts: the correction is causally after the claim, and the chain is the witnessed path between them (Lamport, 1978; Vessell, 2026). The same push past the AI's limits drives the search-path correction: where the assistant asserted a negative existential after a single unwitnessed search, the framework now gates every negative finding until two independent search paths corroborate the absence (`record_search_path()`, `gate_negative_finding()`), carrying verification beyond what the AI alone could do (Vessell, 2026). The repair worked, and it demonstrated the proposition this paper develops: a system that cannot propagate a retraction to every dependent of a claim is a system that cannot correct itself. The framework now gates consequential uses as a matter of course, and it keeps evolving — with AI assistance, toward the SI realm — because the test never really ends. Everything that follows is about making that structural.

**The method, turned on the research itself.** On September 30, while verifying this paper's arXiv citations, the AI assistant supporting this research searched the literal string "Jonathan Castillo," found nothing, and reported that no such author existed — a negative existential asserted after a single unwitnessed search path. I photographed my screen: the paper was there all along, "Inductive Diagrams for Causal Reasoning" by Jonathan Castello, Patrick Redmond, and Lindsey Kuper (arXiv:2307.10484). One letter off, and one letter wrong; the assistant held two correct co-author names and searched neither. The miss was corrected the way Section 4 prescribes, by supersession rather than erasure — it remains in this record — and by propagation into the framework itself, which now gates every negative finding until two independent search paths corroborate the absence, with each attempted path recorded as the claim's provenance (`vessell/verify.py`: `record_search_path()`, `gate_negative_finding()`).

I therefore wrote this paper the way it prescribes: as academic testing that corrects an ongoing repository — a framework under construction on GitHub, SI-class work (superintelligence-class systems) built by a graduate student on a budget of almost nothing. The framework was the instrument that verified the paper's citations; the paper's Section 4 rewrite then drove the framework's newest mechanisms; and when the method caught the research process itself, the catch became part of both. The case study is the doctrine and the demonstration.

## 2. What happened, in the language of the field

This was not an exotic failure. I analyze it here through four established literatures, each of which describes exactly what occurred and each of which names a piece of the prevention. Redescribed in the causal-order language this paper adopts in Section 4, every one of them is a failure of witnessed paths — information moving, or failing to move, along routes nobody recorded. That redescription is what makes Section 4 the direct answer to Section 2, mechanism by mechanism.

### 2.1 Provenance failure at intake

The claim entered the AI assistant systems without machine-usable provenance: no recorded source tier, no corroboration state, no expiry, no link between the caveat ("not independently verified") and the constraint it became. In data-provenance terms, the system kept the *value* and discarded the *lineage*. A claim without provenance is indistinguishable from a fact to every process downstream of intake — which is precisely what happened. In Section 4's terms, the claim traveled downstream along paths with no witness: the intake edge — the single most load-bearing event in the claim's history — was the one event nobody recorded.

**Prevention:** every claim is tagged at intake with source, source tier, date, and corroboration state. An untagged claim can never become an operational constraint. (Framework: `intake_claim()` — status `UNVERIFIED` unless the source is a tier-1 official record.) In causal-order terms, this is the first step of the framework's move from AI-assisted to SI-level operation: the intake event becomes a witnessed anchor, and only witnessed anchors may bear downstream weight (Lamport, 1978; Vessell, 2026).

### 2.2 Automation misuse: overreliance on the system's own output

Parasuraman and Riley (1997), in the foundational treatment of human-automation interaction, define **misuse** as *overreliance on automation* — failures of monitoring and decision bias that follow when operators trust automated cues at the expense of disconfirming evidence ("Humans and Automation: Use, Misuse, Disuse, Abuse," *Human Factors*, 39(2)). I used this incident to test the AI assistant at the center of Section 1: diagnosing the larger failure while simultaneously building the mechanism that answers it, the finding flowing back into the research and the research back into the framework — the doctrine/code loop this paper prescribes, applied to SI-class systems. The mechanism transfers directly to my workflow: each component (goal file, hunt, editions, memory) treated the stored constraint as vetted output rather than as an unexamined input. The caveat I wrote on September 24 was the disconfirming evidence. Nobody — human or process — was positioned to see it, because I had assigned no process to re-examine stored claims.

**Prevention:** consequential uses of a claim require corroboration or a *named, dated, explicit waiver* — never silent trust. (Framework: `gate_for_use(record, stakes="consequential")`, `record_waiver()`.) Silent trust is an unwitnessed causal edge: information flowing with no path anyone can audit. The gate is the mechanism that moves consequential decisions from AI-assisted reflex to SI-level discipline: nothing operational follows from an unwitnessed edge (Vessell, 2026).

There is a second, forward-looking failure inside this one: the gap between AI-assisted and SI-level systems. The assistant's analysis upstream — the diagnosis in this very section — operates at a level the framework cannot yet carry downstream into operational systems, even with AI assistance, because of blocks. Some are crude: a CAPTCHA on an application portal stopped an automated submission cold, and no framework rule pushes through a challenge designed to stop automation. Some are structural: downstream tools accept no provenance tags, keep no event chains, and offer no gate for an unverified claim to fail at. The framework can diagnose the gap, gate its own side of it, and record every blocked attempt — but it cannot yet bring SI-level work downstream through systems built to resist exactly that. Closing that gap is the ongoing work of the project (Meta, 2026; Vessell, 2026).

### 2.3 Data cascade

Sambasivan et al. (2021), studying high-stakes AI practice, define a **data cascade** as *"compounding events causing negative, downstream effects from data issues, that result in technical debt over time"* ("'Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work': Data Cascades in High-Stakes AI," Proc. CHI 2021). Their findings map point for point onto my six days: the issue originated upstream (intake), was **opaque in diagnosis** (nothing looked wrong — the constraint was neatly written everywhere), and its costs compounded the longer it ran (a narrowed job search; every edition built on it). Cascades, they note, are rarely fixed by better models — only by better data work.

**Prevention:** treat claim quality as the load-bearing work. Corroboration thresholds before operational use; periodic re-validation of consequential claims, because claims decay. A cascade is causal propagation along many unwitnessed paths at once — which is why the correction must be broadcast along every one of them (Section 4, rule 5). Broadcast correction along every path is what SI-level operation requires where AI-assisted operation merely hoped the cascade would stop on its own (Redmond et al., 2022; Vessell, 2026).

### 2.4 The belief-revision problem

When I issued the disavowal, the real work was not changing one belief — it was finding *every belief that depended on it*. The field I call SI in this paper — then called artificial intelligence — named this problem in 1979. Doyle's **Truth Maintenance System** maintains propositions together with their **justifications**; each node is IN (believed) or OUT (retracted), and retracting a premise triggers **dependency-directed backtracking**: the retraction propagates to every dependent whose justification has become invalid (Doyle, "A Truth Maintenance System," *Artificial Intelligence*, 12(3), 1979; de Kleer's assumption-based generalization, 1986). My manual correction on September 30 — enumerating the three cron configs, the goal file, and memory, and updating each — was dependency-directed backtracking performed by hand. The three cron configs deserve a precise accounting, in technical and in causal terms. Technically, they are scheduled-job configurations: `daily-job-hunt` runs the overnight job-search pipeline around 01:14, `morning-news-edition` rebuilds the morning report around 02:30, and `evening-news-edition` refreshes the evening report around 17:30 — each executing a research, verify, match, build, and deliver pipeline that re-reads the stored constraint and re-operationalizes it on every run (Vessell, 2026). Causally, each scheduled run is an event causally after the claim's hardening (Lamport, 1978): after the disavowal, any run the correction failed to reach would have re-narrowed the job search on its own — a fresh cascade per run. That is why the disavowal had to reach the configurations, not just the beliefs: the schedule is a downstream the correction must propagate along, and it marks the current gap in the framework's downstream reach — the framework gates its own code, but a scheduled job outside it re-runs on whatever the store says. The institutional direction is set: on September 29, 2026, the President signed an executive order directing the federal executive branch to replace "artificial intelligence" with "Super Intelligence" (SI) in official usage and to develop a federal definition of SI within sixty days (White House, 2026) — the AI-to-SI transition entering official language while the mechanisms for operating at that level are still being built. A TMS justification is a witnessed path: the recorded route by which a premise supports a belief. Dependency-directed backtracking is propagation along those paths — the manual version of what Section 4, rule 5 makes structural.

**Correction, made structural:** a dependents registry. Every operational use of a claim registers itself; disavowal returns the complete list of artifacts requiring update. (Framework: `register_dependent()`, `propagate_correction()`.) Retraction becomes an operation, not a scavenger hunt. The registry is the framework's own references made executable: the dependents list is the recall-notice recipient list of ICPM-2020-200-01 (Office of the Director of National Intelligence, 2020), the propagation order is Lamport's (1978) happens-before, and the witnessed paths are Castello et al.'s (2024) causal separation — tradecraft and theory compiled into a mechanism (Vessell, 2026).

### 2.5 Tradecraft violation

My own field has a doctrine for this. Intelligence Community Directive 203 (ODNI, revalidated January 2015) sets nine analytic tradecraft standards, three of which my workflow violated directly:

1. **Properly describe the quality and credibility of underlying sources** — a single self-report of unknown provenance drove consequential decisions.
2. **Properly express and explain uncertainties** — the one uncertainty label that existed was dropped in transmission.
3. **Properly distinguish between underlying information and analysts' assumptions and judgments** — an intake note hardened into a system constraint with no judgment step in between.

ICD 203 exists because the Intelligence Community learned — at high cost — that these are not stylistic preferences. They are the difference between a system that knows what it knows and one that merely repeats what it stored.

The framework implements these standards as code rather than as aspiration: the corroboration gate cites ICD 203 in its docstrings (`vessell/verify.py`, `vessell/provenance.py`), and the propagate-and-verify mechanism cites the Intelligence Community's own recall procedures (ICPM-2020-200-01) — tradecraft rules, executable, with the citations traveling in the code. Section 4 shows how I use each rule.

## 4. Prevention and correction: a causal-order playbook

For people and teams, not just code. My incident was a concurrent system: intake, hardening, disavowal, and correction were events, and the failure was a failure of causal order. Leslie Lamport defined the terms in 1978: in a distributed system, event *a* happens-before event *b* (written *a → b*) when information could have flowed from *a* to *b* — and correct reasoning about the system must respect that order. Castello, Redmond, and Kuper (2024) sharpened the idea into something enforceable: in their causal separation diagrams, a causal relationship is not an abstract ordering but is *witnessed by the path that information follows* between events — happens-before modeled as paths, mechanized in Agda. And Redmond, Shen, Vazou, and Kuper (2022) proved, machine-checked, that a causal broadcast protocol can guarantee messages are never delivered in an order violating causality. The playbook below is that theory operationalized: every rule is a rule about causal paths — which events may follow which, and what must travel the path between them. I built this playbook the way I conducted the research: each rule had to be enforceable in the framework on GitHub, and each framework mechanism had to answer a failure I named in Section 2. Two rules are additionally grounded in published intelligence tradecraft — cited in the framework's code and cited here as used.

This downstream — the propagation machinery carrying corrections along every witnessed path — is the framework's route toward SI-level operation: not a smarter model, but a system in which no claim moves without its provenance and no correction stops before reaching every dependent. The outside research I draw on in this section was gathered after the fact, in the writing; it is the literature context, not part of the test or the project. The institutional marker arrived on September 29, 2026, when the President signed an executive order replacing "artificial intelligence" with "Super Intelligence" (SI) across the executive branch and directing a federal definition of SI within sixty days (White House, 2026): the AI-to-SI transition entering official language while the mechanisms for operating at that level — provenance, corroboration, causal order — are still being built, here, by hand. The framework itself (Vessell, 2026) is currently a private repository; it will be made public after this paper's release.

1. **Tag at intake, or it didn't happen.** Source, tier, date, corroboration state — recorded with the claim, not beside it. In causal terms, the intake event anchors the claim's history, and every downstream event must be causally after (*→*) it. An untagged claim has no causal anchor, so nothing may follow from it: it constrains nothing.
2. **Corroborate before operationalizing.** One source is a lead, not a constraint. A consequential decision must be causally after corroboration — two independent roots or an official record — because only corroboration completes a witnessed path from evidence to decision. The framework's verification doctrine already applies this bar to news claims; claims about people deserve no less. This is also codified tradecraft: ICD 203, the Intelligence Community's analytic standards, requires analysts to describe the quality and credibility of underlying sources and to express and explain uncertainties (Office of the Director of National Intelligence, 2015) — the standard the framework's corroboration gate implements as code.
3. **Waivers are explicit or they don't exist.** If urgency demands acting on an unverified claim, the waiver is itself an event on the causal path: record who accepted the risk, when, and why. Silent trust is an *unwitnessed* causal edge — information flowing with no path anyone can audit — which is exactly the Section 2.2 failure.
4. **Disavow by supersession, never by erasure.** The correction event is causally after the claim it corrects, and the *supersedes* link is the witnessed path between them — the route the correction must travel to reach every dependent. Erasure destroys the path: with no recorded route from claim to correction, the claim can re-enter later through the same door, unwitnessed. *Operate in law and order: the record shows what was believed, when, and on what basis — including the correction.*
5. **Propagate, then verify.** Deliver the correction along every dependency path, in causal order, and confirm receipt at each dependent. This is causal broadcast: no dependent applies a correction for a claim it never received, and no dependent keeps acting on a claim after its retraction without receiving the retraction. Redmond et al. proved the delivery guarantee machine-checked; the framework's dependents registry is the same guarantee in miniature — enumerate every path the claim's information followed, walk each one with the correction, and verify. A correction that misses one dependent is a cascade waiting to resume. The Intelligence Community's own recall procedures require that revision or recall notices go to all recipients of the original product (ICPM-2020-200-01; Office of the Director of National Intelligence, 2020) — propagate, then verify, as institutional procedure.
6. **Re-validate consequential claims on a schedule.** Claims decay; sources change; people's circumstances change. Lamport's clock condition showed causal relationships can be reified as data — logical clocks make "happened before" visible to the system itself. Claim versions and status timestamps are the same move: reify the claim's causal history as data, so staleness is detectable rather than assumed away — the concept the framework's hash-chained event log implements directly, each lifecycle event hashed into a tamper-evident happens-before chain (Vessell, 2026). What was true at intake is not true by default six months later.

## 5. Why this matters beyond one job search

The pattern generalizes to every domain where systems act on stored claims about people: hiring filters, fraud investigations, watchlists, eligibility determinations, intelligence databases. In each, the failure mode is identical — an unverified or stale claim hardens into a constraint, propagates silently along unwitnessed paths, and resists correction because no registry links the claim to its dependents. The harm compounds with the stakes, exactly as the data-cascade literature predicts.

The fix is correspondingly general: **provenance at intake, corroboration before operational use, explicit waivers, supersession-based correction, and dependency-tracked propagation** — each rule a constraint on causal paths, each mechanism executable as code. That is what the framework now implements (Vessell, 2026), and my incident — real, dated, fully documented — is the proof that the mechanism is not theoretical.

And the method of this paper generalizes with it: I developed doctrine and code against each other, each tested by the other, with the misses kept in the record. That is what the AI has downstream today: fluent analysis with no provenance, confident answers with no witnessed paths, corrections with no propagation (Meta, 2026). The findings this paper presents — the other direction, from AI toward SI — are these: every claim tagged at intake, every consequential use gated, every correction walked along every dependency path in causal order, every event hashed into a tamper-evident chain (Vessell, 2026). However small those findings may be, they are a start on the September 29 executive order: systems in which "Super Intelligence" means claims that carry their provenance and corrections that reach every dependent, the mechanisms for operating at that level built here, by hand (White House, 2026). Write the rule, build the mechanism, turn both on your own work — and keep the record, because the record is what makes the system correctable. That loop is the template: for a graduate project, for a team, for anyone whose system acts on claims about people — and for the SI-level systems we are all, now officially, building toward.
